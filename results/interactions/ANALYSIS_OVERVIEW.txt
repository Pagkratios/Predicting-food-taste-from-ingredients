================================================================================
CROSS-MODAL TASTE INTERACTION ANALYSIS -- FULL OVERVIEW
Response to reviewer comment (1): "Lack of cross-modal interaction terms"
================================================================================

REVIEWER COMMENT
----------------
"The model does not account for perceptual taste interactions, such as sweetness
suppressing bitterness, saltiness enhancing umami, or fat enhancing sweetness.
In recipes containing high levels of sugar and salt, this may lead to noticeable
errors."

Three named mechanisms plus one falsifiable prediction about where errors should
appear. All four are testable. This document records the tests and every number.


================================================================================
1. DATA
================================================================================

Source            data/raw_recipes.py (only input; no trained artifacts read)
Recipes           70
Unique ingredients 115
Taste dimensions  sweet, bitter, salty, umami, sour  (0-100 scale)

Three matrices are built:

  profiles  (115 x 5)  each unique ingredient's raw sensory scores
  mixture   (70 x 5)   weight-normalised average of ingredient scores
                       = the ADDITIVE, INTERACTION-FREE prediction
  measured  (70 x 5)   the panel's actual recipe scores

Chemistry features (same regexes as Hybrid/src/hybrid_analysis.py) used in the
control analyses: protein_frac, sugar_frac, maillard_potential, salt_frac,
water_frac, conc_factor, allium_frac, fermented_frac.

Coverage of the derived composition features:
  sugar_frac   median 0.010   max 0.504   non-zero in 39/70 recipes
  salt_frac    median 0.010   max 0.050   non-zero in 57/70 recipes
  fat_frac     median 0.200   max 0.950   non-zero in 63/70 recipes
  (fat proxy: butter|oil|cream|margarine|mayonnaise|lard|ghee|coconut milk)


================================================================================
2. METHOD
================================================================================

2.1 THE ADDITIVE BASELINE
The five mixture taste values are z-scored, then an ordinary linear regression
maps them to each measured taste. Coefficients are found by least squares:
minimise the sum of squared errors over the training recipes.

2.2 RESIDUALS
Every prediction is LEAVE-ONE-OUT: recipe i is predicted by a model fit on the
other 69. Residual = measured - out-of-sample prediction, in raw 0-100 points.
Positive = the recipe tasted stronger than the sum of its ingredients.

Why LOO matters: in-sample residuals are mechanically orthogonal to the fitted
features and shrunk toward zero, so finding no structure would prove nothing.
LOO residuals are genuine predictive failures and are free to correlate with
whatever actually drives them.

2.3 INTERACTION TERMS
All 10 pairwise products of the z-scored mixture features:
  sweet x bitter, sweet x salty, sweet x umami, sweet x sour, bitter x salty,
  bitter x umami, bitter x sour, salty x umami, salty x sour, umami x sour

Because the inputs are centred, a product is a CO-DEVIATION score, not an
intensity: positive when both channels are unusually high OR both unusually low,
negative when they pull apart. Without centring the product would merely track
overall recipe intensity.

2.4 THE TEST LOGIC
If perception multiplies two channels, the additive model should fail exactly
where that product is large. So: correlate each residual against each product
term. 5 residuals x 10 products = 50 tests, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR corrected.

2.5 POWER
At N = 70, residual-interaction correlations are detectable only down to
|r| ~ 0.33 (80% power, alpha = 0.05, two-sided). Smaller effects cannot be
excluded by these analyses.

3.5  DIRECT TESTS OF THE THREE NAMED MECHANISMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) SWEETNESS SUPPRESSING BITTERNESS
    If true, recipes with more sugar should taste LESS bitter than predicted
    -> negative correlation between bitterness residual and sugar.

    resid_bitter vs sugar_frac                r = -0.004   p = 0.974
    resid_bitter vs mixture sweetness         r = -0.008   p = 0.949
    resid_bitter vs sugar, bitter > 0 (n=65)  r = -0.027   p = 0.830
    residual sweet-bitter correlation         r = -0.007   p = 0.958
    sweet x bitter term on bitter residual    r = +0.03
    single-term LOO RMSE, bitterness       1.256 -> 1.343   (+0.087 WORSE)

    VERDICT: no detectable signature.

(b) FAT-ASSOCIATED SWEETNESS ENHANCEMENT
    If true, fat-rich recipes should taste SWEETER than predicted
    -> positive correlation between sweetness residual and fat.

    resid_sweet vs fat_frac                   r = -0.214   p = 0.076
    resid_sweet vs fat x mixture sweetness    r = -0.040   p = 0.743
    resid_sweet vs fat x sugar_frac           r = -0.073   p = 0.546

    VERDICT: not significant, and the point estimate runs OPPOSITE to the
    hypothesis (fat-rich recipes are if anything over-predicted for sweetness).

(c) SALTINESS-UMAMI ENHANCEMENT
    resid_salty vs resid_umami                r = +0.616   p = 1.4e-08
    salty x umami term vs resid_salty         r = -0.227   p = 0.058
    salty x umami term vs resid_umami         r = -0.053   p = 0.662
    single-term LOO RMSE, umami            8.365 -> 8.492   (+0.128 WORSE)

    VERDICT: the residuals genuinely co-move, but the MULTIPLICATIVE term
    predicts neither. Combined with the ingredient-level correlation (r = 0.511),
    this is a SHARED-MAGNITUDE ERROR -- both attributes under-predicted in the
    same recipes -- consistent with evaporative concentration and cooking
    chemistry, not with receptor-level synergy.

3.8  NESTED SELECTION -- NO LEAKAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Choosing a term by looking at the LOO results and then reporting the LOO
improvement is grading against the answer key. The honest protocol selects the
term inside each fold using ONLY the 69 training recipes, then predicts the
held-out recipe. "None" is an allowed choice.

  target   additive  cherry-picked  NESTED-HONEST   delta   term chosen (of 70)
  sweet       9.965         9.151         9.611    -0.353   bitterxumami (69)
  salty      12.021        11.430        11.430    -0.591   bitterxumami (70)
  sour        9.820         8.836         8.836    -0.983   sweetxsour (70)
  bitter      1.256         1.261         1.312    +0.056   NONE (63)
  umami       8.365         8.296         8.542    +0.177   sweetxumami (58),
                                                            saltyxsour (12)

  3 of 5 improve under a protocol with no selection leakage.

  Evidence the method is behaving correctly:
   - For salty and sour the nested and cherry-picked numbers are IDENTICAL,
     because the same term was chosen in all 70 folds -- selection bias was zero.
   - For bitterness the selector DECLINES in 63 of 70 folds, correctly
     identifying that there is nothing to add.
   - Umami is the one that got worse, and it is the one where selection was
     unstable (58 folds vs 12). Disagreement across folds -> no real effect.
   - Sweetness shows the leakage premium: cherry-picked 9.151 vs honest 9.611.
     The real gain is 0.353, not 0.814.

================================================================================
4. CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

1. The additive model already absorbs nearly all recipe-level taste correlation.
   Sweet-bitter goes from +0.363 in measured recipes to -0.007 in residuals.
   Apparent taste correlation is inherited from ingredient co-occurrence and
   culinary archetype, not from perceptual interaction.

2. There is essentially no interaction structure left to find. 2 of 50 tests
   survive FDR; max |r| = 0.437; neither survivor matches a described mechanism.

3. NONE of the reviewer's three named mechanisms leaves a detectable signature,
   and the two that are testable make prediction WORSE even when added alone.

4. The reviewer's error prediction fails. High-sugar/high-salt recipes show no
   elevated error (p = 0.489), and the sugar x salt product never predicts error.
   The apparent sugar-error link is heteroscedasticity and vanishes once the
   measured level is controlled for.

5. Ten interaction terms at once badly overfit at N = 70 (sweetness +39% RMSE).
   Two-thirds of the fitted interaction coefficients are smaller than their own
   fold-to-fold wobble.

6. Selected honestly one at a time, three of five attributes improve -- but two
   of those three collapse once controlled against chemistry features already in
   the hybrid model. They were proxying sugar content.

7. EXACTLY ONE effect survives every control: bitter x umami improves saltiness
   prediction from 11.222 to 10.249 with chemistry included, selected in 70/70
   folds. It correlates with allium content (+0.357) and inversely with sugar
   (-0.356), so it behaves as a NONLINEAR COMPOSITION/ARCHETYPE TERM for savoury
   cooked dishes, NOT as a cross-modal perceptual interaction. It should be
   reported as exploratory: it emerged from a search over 10 terms x 5 targets
   with several control sets, on a single dataset of 70 recipes.

8. Power caveat: at N = 70 only |r| >~ 0.33 is detectable. Weaker interactions
   cannot be excluded.
